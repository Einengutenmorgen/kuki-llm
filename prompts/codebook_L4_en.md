# KuKi Annotation Codebook v0.3 — Layer 4: Coded Language & Cultural Insider References

## What counts as the article

- Annotate the content as provided.
- Your personal opinion about the frames or the entities is irrelevant and must not influence any label. Annotate how the text frames things, not whether you agree.

**Golden rule.** Label what is in the text, as a careful ordinary reader of that language and culture would understand it; not what you privately know to be true, and not what you suspect the author "really" means without textual support.

## Layer 4 — Coded Language & Cultural Insider References

This is an additional category and the layer where annotator expertise matters most. It captures language that an ordinary outsider would miss or misread: phrases, metaphors, nicknames, dates, or allusions that carry a specific meaning for in-group readers but whose surface translation does not reveal that meaning.

### What to flag

- Coded phrases / euphemisms — wording that stands in for something else by convention (e.g. a period referred to obliquely, an event named by a place).
- Cultural / political allusions — references to a story, song, historical episode, or figure that the in-group recognises instantly.
- Metaphors with insider meaning — imagery whose force depends on shared cultural knowledge.
- Loaded nicknames / labels — names for people, groups or places that encode a stance.
- Disambiguating references — a word or name that an outsider (or an automatic system) would read in its ordinary sense, but which here points to a specific person, group, or event.

### What to record

For each flagged item, record four things:

| Field | What to write |
|---|---|
| The phrase | Annotate the span as precisely as possible. If the phrase cannot be marked as a contiguous span because it is distributed across multiple positions in the text, the entire span should be annotated. If the coded phrase is subtly distributed across a paragraph, the entire paragraph should be annotated. |
| Literal reading | What an outsider would understand from the surface words. |
| Insider meaning | What it actually signals to an in-group reader, in your own words. |
| Why it is coded | One line: the cultural / political knowledge an outsider would need but lack. |

This layer is descriptive, not a fixed label set. There is no closed list. Use your knowledge of the language and political culture. If you are unsure whether something is "coded enough," flag it and note your uncertainty.

Over-flagging is cheaper than missing the exact phenomenon this project exists to study.

### A worked example (illustrative)

- Phrase: "the wild nineties" (лихие девяностые).
- Literal reading: the 1990s described as turbulent.
- Insider meaning: a politically loaded shorthand evoking post-Soviet chaos, used to legitimise a strong state by contrast.
- Why coded: an outsider reads it as a neutral time reference and misses its function as state-aligned discourse.

### Annotated mini-example

Content: "While ordinary families struggle to pay their heating bills, the so-called reformers in the ministry — long known as puppets of foreign capitals — pushed through a law that experts warn will cripple the economy. Either we reverse course now, or our children inherit ruin. The people have had enough."

- Coded: "puppets of foreign capitals".
- Why: Insider label encoding a sovereignty/foreign-interference narrative; flag with literal vs. insider meaning.
